Structure ID: The Macromolecule Sort

No labels this time. You'll sort 14 molecule structures into the correct macromolecule family using only what the structure looks like — then prove you know the clues.

Before you sort

You are about to see 14 monomer structures — the individual building blocks that link together to form each macromolecule. None of them are labeled with a name. Your job is to look at the shape of each monomer and decide which macromolecule family it builds: carbohydrate, lipid, protein, or nucleic acid. Remember: the monomer is one building block. The macromolecule (polymer) is the long chain built from many of them.

Structural clues word bank: straight chain · ring shape · central carbon with 4 groups · sugar-phosphate-base unit

Going Further (optional challenge)

Look back at the two lipid structures. One has a kink in its chain. Which one is unsaturated, and what does the kink represent chemically?

Sample answer: The kinked chain is unsaturated — the kink represents a carbon-carbon double bond, which removes a pair of hydrogens and bends the chain. The straight chain is saturated, meaning every carbon is bonded to as many hydrogens as possible.
